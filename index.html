How Virginia Tech calculates your GPA

VT grade points

There is no A+ at Virginia Tech. P, W, and I grades carry no points; an I that isn't finished in time turns into an F.

Is QCA the same as GPA?

Yes. VT long called it QCA (quality credit average), and many students and advisors still do. The university's grading policy now uses "GPA" throughout, but the math is the same: total quality points divided by GPA hours attempted at VT.

How are quality points figured?

Multiply each course's credits by its grade points. A B+ in a 4-credit course is 4 × 3.3 = 13.2 quality points. Add them up and divide by your A–F graded hours. Pass/fail and continuing (X) courses are left out entirely.

Does retaking a class replace the old grade?

No. VT has no grade replacement. Both attempts count in your GPA. You can only retake a course to raise a C- or lower, and you get three attempts at most. This is the biggest way generic GPA calculators get VT wrong.

What does Dean's List take?

At least 12 credit hours graded A–F in a fall or spring semester and a term GPA of 3.4 or higher. Pass/fail hours don't count toward the 12, and there's no summer Dean's List.

Graduating with distinction

You need at least 60 hours completed at VT and an overall GPA of 3.4 or higher: cum laude from 3.4, magna cum laude from 3.6, summa cum laude from 3.8. The GPA is not rounded for this, so a 3.598 is cum laude, not magna.

Academic standing

A cumulative GPA of 2.0 or higher is good standing. A term GPA under 2.0 with a cumulative still at 2.0 or above puts you on academic warning. A cumulative under 2.0 means academic probation, which is reviewed after each fall and spring. You also need a 2.0 overall to graduate.

Can I take a class pass/fail to protect my GPA?

Only for free electives (not major requirements or Pathways courses), once you have 30 completed hours and at least a 2.5 GPA, with a limit of two per term. A D- or lower becomes an F, which does count.
